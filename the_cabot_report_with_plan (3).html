Implementation Plan

What we'd actually build

Three connected systems, sequenced so the foundation lands first and the hero compounds on top of it. Each is described by what we're building, why it matters, and what it replaces or improves.

System 1 · Hero

Marketing & Communications Production Engine

The upgraded booking-to-ticket-sales system for The Cabot. Two phases that mirror how the calendar actually runs: offer decisions (which acts to make offers on, for which dates, at what economics, with more complete data behind each call) and show marketing & ticket sales (driving the right audience to each confirmed show through tuned marketing and ad spend across the recurring comms work). The Phase 1 build is the more immediate, data-driven piece; Phase 2 grows from it. The first 60 days post-build are where the engine gets tuned – Crane runs real offer decisions and real marketing campaigns through both phases alongside your marketing person, calibrating the system against actual results. After the ramp period, Crane continues operating the engine alongside your team as a dedicated force multiplier – with handover to your marketing person when you decide the system is firing on all cylinders and the timing is right. When it goes in-house, we transfer everything so the marketing person can run it without us.

What we're building

  • Phase 1 · Offer Decisions: a decision-support layer for the booking team that pulls historical Cabot sell-through, Prism Insights, social and streaming signals, and the updated genre-tagged data into one place – so offer decisions on which acts to book for which dates are made with consistent, complete inputs rather than scattered lookups. Brian's experience and gut still lead the call – the system adds data behind those decisions.
  • Phase 2 · Show Marketing & Ticket Sales: once acts are confirmed, ad spend tuned per show (not flat across every booking), audience targeting against the cleaned-up genre data, and the full comms set (announcement, member presale, sponsor pitch, community program flyers, social, the Friday weekly note).
  • Cadence and scheduling logic for promoting overlapping shows – coordinating the 6–8 shows on sale at any given time and the ones happening that week, so each gets the right airtime in the right window across channels
  • The show-launch handoff (booker → marketing → box office) runs through a shared workflow instead of separate emails and conversations. Show details get entered once and everyone downstream works from the same source – faster from booking to on-sale, no information dropped in handoffs.

Why it matters

  • ~300 events a year is too much for one marketing person to run while also dialing in a new system. Every show is a different artist, audience, and message – hundreds of tailored campaigns, not one repeated over and over. The 60-day fractional ramp gives the marketing person room to learn the system without being asked to also operate it while it's still being calibrated.
  • Spend allocation today is flat: $4,000 across every show, whether it's a $100K booking that will sell out anyway or a $15,000 show that needs the budget. The intelligence layer recommends ad spend, audience targeting, and marketing approach per show, and the operational layer puts the campaign in motion.
  • Captures the pattern recognition Brian has built over 20 years and puts it inside a system the booking side can use, rather than living in one head.

What it replaces

  • Flat ad-spend defaults applied across very different shows
  • Repetitive comms work currently produced one show at a time without templates or workflow support
  • The show-launch handoff happening as a chain of individual touches
  • Pattern recognition that only lives in Brian's head
System 2 · Foundation

Cabot AI Workspace

The team-level equivalent of what Brian has built personally. The right platform selection informed by current team usage, personal-account projects rebuilt as multi-user, and process and guardrails for using Spectrix data with AI safely. This is the platform underneath everything else – the call confirmed it as the day-one starting point.

What we're building

  • Platform decision takes into account how the team currently uses AI – Brian's Claude work, the team's ChatGPT habits, and Microsoft Copilot all factor in, so the selection fits the actual work patterns already in place
  • Personal-account projects rebuilt as multi-user workspaces – with a clear owner on the team for keeping each one current as the work evolves
  • Process and guardrails for using Spectrix data with AI safely – so the donor-data hygiene Brian currently does by hand has a documented, repeatable approach the team can follow
  • Connector and integration evaluation (Prism Insights, Prism's MD beta, whatever connector betas Brian is tracking)
  • Context loaded once: voice, programming calendar, membership tiers, brand standards, show history – so workflows operate against The Cabot's actual context, not a generic AI setup

Why it matters

  • Personal AI accounts learn from everything happening on them – including non-work use. When the account that drafts a donor email is also being used personally or by a family member, the AI's context gets muddied and the work output suffers. Team accounts keep work context clean.
  • Lets the rest of the team get the same kind of leverage Brian has on his personal account – without each person rebuilding it from scratch
  • One workspace that holds the data is the alternative to bolting on point solutions one at a time

What it replaces

  • Personal AI accounts being used for work, with the data hygiene risks that creates
  • Manual donor-data PII stripping in Excel before AI ever touches it
  • Ad-hoc tooling decisions made one team at a time
System 3 · Important

SOP Capture & Operations Playbook

Documenting how the work actually gets done across the team – starting with the roughly 8 of 20 key people whose institutional knowledge isn't written down anywhere. Done with AI-assisted interviews and light surveys, not by asking each person to write up their own day.

What we're building

  • AI-assisted interview and survey workflows that extract operating procedures from the team without putting the burden of write-up on them
  • A documented playbook of the work where institutional knowledge actually lives – the member and donor flows, the Big Night production cycle, sponsor activations, community program intake. (Show-day operations already run as rinse-and-repeat.)
  • Documentation that stays current because it's part of the workflow – not a static document that gets out of date the moment it's written
  • Priority sequencing: the procedures most exposed if a key person leaves get captured first

Why it matters

  • The org is in transition – roles shuffling, responsibilities shifting, "going from a small organization to the next level." It's the right time to get documentation in place.
  • Reduces the key-person exposure across the team without slowing anyone down
  • Feeds back into the AI Workspace – documented procedures become the context the workspace runs on

What it replaces

  • "People just know what to do" as the operating model
  • The risk of institutional knowledge walking out the door if a key person leaves
  • The implicit ask that staff document their own work in addition to doing it

What "AI Readiness" actually means

AI Readiness isn't about whether your team uses ChatGPT. It's about whether the work you actually do is set up to benefit from AI – so it saves time, raises the quality your team produces, and gives you back the hours for the leadership, programming, and community work only your senior team can do.

Five conditions matter:

  • Your data is usable. AI can't work with information that lives only in someone's head.
  • You know where your time is going. Repetitive tasks you can name are tasks you can delegate.
  • You know your customers and community. Who they are, what they need, and how they find you.
  • There's a clear way you do things. AI amplifies what's repeatable. If your methods live in one person's head, there's nothing to amplify yet.
  • You have momentum. You've tried things, you're curious, and you want to move.
